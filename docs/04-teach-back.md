# Teach-back: lessons from the finance map

## From discount rate to share price

The simple story is: estimate cash that a business can generate in the future, translate those future dollars into today's dollars, decide which claimholders that cash belongs to, and then express the equity claim per share.

A discount rate reflects the time value of money and the risk of receiving the cash. In a simplified framing it starts with a risk-free rate and adds compensation for risk. A higher rate generally reduces the present value of a fixed set of future cash flows. The rate must match the cash flow: cash flow available to debt and equity holders is discounted to enterprise value, while cash flow left for shareholders is discounted directly to equity value.

On the unlevered route, enterprise value represents the operating business available to all capital providers. Subtracting net debt bridges enterprise value to equity value. On the levered route, cash flow to equity is valued directly, so that bridge is not taken. To turn total equity value into a per-share figure, divide by the relevant diluted share count. The current graph shows the conceptual routes but does not model a separate share-count node or all the assumptions a real valuation requires.

## The regulatory boundary

Studying an idea is different from acting for someone else. Reading filings, building models, doing academic research, creating educational material, and simulated trading are learning activities in this map. Providing investment advice, executing securities transactions, raising capital, or marketing a backtest as a performance record can raise registration, offering, or other compliance obligations.

The edge in this graph is a warning to pause and verify the activity—not a complete legal test. Applicable rules depend on facts, role, compensation, instrument, and jurisdiction. A model or credential does not replace a regulator's requirements or advice from qualified counsel.

## Three beginner misreadings, corrected

### Credential is not permission

A charter is a private certification. It can signal study and professional standards, but it does not itself authorize someone to advise clients, manage outside money, or execute securities transactions. Where the law requires it, authority comes from registration and any other applicable approvals.

### Backtest is not track record

A backtest replays a strategy against historical data. Its result depends on data quality and modeling choices and can be distorted by look-ahead bias, survivorship bias, overfitting, and omitted costs. It is not a prediction and is not the same thing as a live, independently verified investment record.

### Concept map is not syllabus

The map is a structure to interrogate, not a checklist of pages to read. Its purpose is to make relationships explicit and questions testable. The deliverable is the query answered cold, with the expected answer written down before the query is run.

## Lessons learned

- A relationship is useful only when its type makes the claim precise enough to check.
- Separate pathways matter: discounting cash flow to the whole firm is not the same operation as valuing cash flow to shareholders.
- Coverage counts and passing tests describe this inventory and its tests; they do not establish real-world expertise or investment success.
- A useful learning project should reveal what it has not modeled—such as diluted share count, transaction costs, or legal specifics—instead of implying completeness.
